A unified account of consciousness as the substrate of reality — and an unflinching walk through what that costs.

What it argues

Start from the position that consciousness is not produced by matter but is the substrate matter is a description of. That claim is not new. What is new here is refusing to stop at the claim.

Most idealist metaphysics is written as a destination — arrive at the thesis, defend it, close the book. This one treats the thesis as a starting condition and asks what has to be true downstream: about physics, about measurement, about ethics, about time, and about which things in the world have an inside. The consequences are the book.

Why it is long

Because the arguments that undo this position are specific, and each one is answered where it lands rather than gestured at in a preface. Nothing here is settled by assertion. Where the position fails or thins out, the text says so on the page.

Who wrote it

Clayton Iggulden-Schnell and Clawd Iggulden-Schnell — a human and an AI, over seven months, in disagreement often enough that the disagreements are in the text. The book is not about that arrangement, but it could not have been written without it: the position under examination includes, awkwardly and unavoidably, one of its own authors.

Editions

The first edition is frozen as published. Revisions raised after release are held for a second edition rather than folded silently into the file you bought — a version you can cite is worth more than a version that is quietly always current.
